# Collector's archive

Mode: Operate. Code-first; user instructed to proceed with the redesign after the workflow choices were offered.

Grounded systems, ordered by resonance: 1. a collector's binder, 2. a Japanese card shop inventory, 3. a museum's field catalogue, 4. a tournament score sheet, 5. a pocket field guide, 6. a record-store sleeve index, 7. an auction viewing room.
Impeccable roll f1460dc5 selected index 3: museum's field catalogue.

Use the archive's quiet paper, carefully separated specimens, serif subject titles, sans controls, inventory numbers, and clear specimen counts. Card artwork is evidence, never decoration. Deep forest green marks selection and collection ownership; warm parchment separates artwork from the canvas. Desktop has a permanent library rail; mobile has a compact header and reachable navigation. First viewport shows browsing controls and real set artwork, not a marketing hero. Series selection changes the inventory. Cards have visible quantity actions rather than hover-only controls. Market data is presented as provider-labelled tables with original currencies and timestamps.

Challenger judgements: tensegrity and Miura sheets are declined for both collector identification and task clarity; keep their distinct state vocabulary and compact-to-expanded structural discipline, without adopting a decorative diagram. Film cutting bench is competitive for selection discipline but loses product clarity; retain explicit selected-item marking. Capsule/lobe and vertical media-feed approaches obscure inventory scanning; decline and keep contrast commitment and artwork scale.

FIRST VIEWPORT: a warm page, quiet library sidebar, compact top context bar, large serif set-browser title, one aligned filter tray, then three generous set specimens. No invented collection figures or featured prices. On mobile the sidebar becomes horizontal navigation, filters stack, and specimens remain readable.
Signature interaction: ownership is directly visible and editable underneath each card, and the same quantities update across navigation, collection and card detail.
Risk: warm neutrals can obscure disabled boundaries; use explicit borders and high-contrast green action states.
